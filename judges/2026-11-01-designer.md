**Verdict.** The measured geometry is now excellent (outlines hug the photos). The look is not real: it reads as a clean 2012 game level beside the real photos, and well below the SE Landscapes bar and the Airbnb "lighting does the work" bar. Geometry is no longer the problem; planting, light and surface life are.

## Renderer limits vs asset faults

The renderer will lift light, glass, shadow softness and the aliasing on leaf cards. It will not fix the hedge shape, cone pots, one-species planting, wrong tree, wrong roof colour, flat timber or weathering-free materials. Those are the pile that stays unreal in Unreal.

## Top 5 changes, in order

1. **Fix the lighting and sky.** Unreal Lumen or Cycles, a site HDRI, sun direction taken from the photos, soft bounce, a warm grade. This raises every area at once and is what the Airbnb benchmark says does the work.
2. **Stop hand-modelling the existing living things.** Replace the capsule hedge, cone pots, stock apple tree and spike-planting with captured assets (splat or scan). See the recommendation below.
3. **Build a real planting system for the designed proposals** (SE bar): species-correct, photo-textured plants in drifts, 8-12 per m2, mulch covered, varied height, climbers on the fence, a mature multi-stem tree.
4. **A weathering and colour-sampling pass:** roof (grey, moss), mortar (pale grey), gravel (warm), fence grain, shed paint, joint dirt, lawn mowing and clover.
5. **Clutter and surroundings:** scooter, bike, shoes, bins, the real pots and bench; street hedges, cars, neighbouring houses, in place of the flat green plane.

## Should the existing garden rely on the Gaussian splat?

**Yes, for everything living and organic**: planting, hedge, apple tree, lawn, gravel, weathered shed, roof, neighbours, sky edge, clutter. That is what the splat is good at, and Cameron Cone's bar (benchmark 4) shows it reads as real. Hand-modelling planting will not match it (the Airbnb flat has no plants to compare).

Keep the measured model for what a splat is bad at: collision and walk mode, the clean base (fixed space only), proposals sitting in the scene, and sharp-edged hard surfaces such as paving, house frame and door.

Caveats: expect blur and floaters on foliage and in the tree crown, and the phone-grade input is below Cameron's bar (benchmark 4 says so). Aim: splat for "today", the modelled planting library only for "designed" proposals.
