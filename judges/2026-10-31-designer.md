# Judge 1 — garden designer vs the SE Landscapes film (31 Oct, after scanned trees + planting)

Verdict: the two scanned trees are the most convincing thing in frame; everything else still
reads as a 2010 game level. Would not show a client these stills.

1. Wrong plant palette, far too sparse — build UK border perennials procedurally in Blender
   geometry nodes (lavender, allium, verbena bonariensis, ammi/achillea umbels, stipa, box
   balls); 7–9 perennials/m², drifts of 5–9 on a Poisson fill, mulch texture under all.
2. Leftover card foliage everywhere (stage-6 shrubs, neighbour trees, hedge) — delete; neighbour
   trees as instanced scans beyond the fence, hazed. Biggest instant win.
3. Fence flat brown — real cedar boards 95×20 with 12 mm gaps, posts, capping, per-board hue.
4. No design: rectangle lawn — circular lawn ~4.6 m with steel edge, beds 1.8–2.5 m, gravel
   path with stepping stones, trees moved into the beds framing the studio.
5. Lawn sparse/blotchy; blades poke through deck and spur — avoid lists, denser short blades or
   texture-led lawn, lift tint to ~0x9fc06c.
6. Midday hard and saturated — partly-cloudy HDRI, soft shadows (VSM/PCSS), GTAO on desktop
   tiers r 0.9, contact decals under plants on Quest, MSAA through the composer, gentle grade.
7. Golden hour just darker — sun y≈6.5 over the fence, exposure 1.25, hemi 0.6.
8. Night has no lighting plan — darker sky dome, 2700 K uplighters per tree, spike lights,
   bollards, soft interior area light, night-only bloom; bake for Quest.
9. Materials — deck not red plastic, spur paving at real slab size, flag variation, mulch.
10. House — real glazing, roof, reveals; neighbours too (temporary until the capture).
11. Trees twinned and Mediterranean — one island tree max; multi-stem birch/amelanchier built
    in Blender with pale bark and small leaves, 30% sky through the canopy.
12. Camera grammar — stills/film at 0.9–1.1 m, 35 mm, foreground planting, slow dollies.

Single change: tear out every card object and replant 2 m beds with procedural perennials.
